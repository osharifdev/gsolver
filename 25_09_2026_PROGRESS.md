# Self-certifying GBP: progress log, 25 September 2026

Goal (restated by the user, 14:43): per-pose error bounds that come directly from GBP, valid, and tight enough to let SLAM stop computing early (early exit), robust enough for a CVPR paper. The stopping rule is fixed by the brief: pose i may stop when its certified error bound E_i ≤ η · S_lo_i (η = 0.1), where S_lo_i is a certified lower bound on the pose's own statistical error radius (δ = 0.05). "Directly from GBP" here means computed from the model GBP runs on (its factors) and from GBP's current state, through its residual and local solves; nothing needs ground truth or the global solution.

**What I think is going on**
- ChArUco is recorded at 30 fps. The GP prior couples velocities stiffly, 1/(dt·Q_c) with dt = 1/30 s, while the markers observe only poses. - The board markers are hubs seen by most frames, so every patch boundary touches dozens of exterior poses. That inflates the leak coefficients a_ij, even though the exact leak is small: C3's local share is 1.0 when K covers the graph.
- In short, the local certificates are right, but the coefficient bounds throw away cancellation and directional structure. That is where a paper-worthy improvement would come from.

## 15:12 · A prediction for stereo, written before seeing its numbers

A scalar toy of bundle adjustment:
- pose i observes L landmarks, each with information w;
- every landmark is seen by n_o poses;
- the K = 1 patch is pose i with its landmarks.

Then [H_PP⁻¹]_il = [H_PP⁻¹]_ii · w/(n_o·w), and the C3 coefficients to the other observers sum to **exactly 1**, for any L and any n_o. Reason: conditional on its landmarks a pose is exact, so its error is a weighted average of the neighbours' errors carried through the shared landmarks. Row sums of A (and so ρ(A)) drop below 1 only by the share of the pose's information that is *absolute*. Here that is the single-observation landmarks, eliminated into unary factors, about 26 per pose against about 290 multi-observation landmarks: roughly 8 %.

So on stereo I expect ρ(A) ≈ 1 at K = 1 and slow improvement with K. This is the local face of the global fact that BA errors are dominated by smooth, drift-like modes.

Two consequences worth testing:
1. **The stopping threshold scales with absolute uncertainty.** S_lo uses the marginal covariance, which contains the drift uncertainty. For poses far from well-determined regions it is large (μ_lo ≫ 1). A certificate that is valid but inflated by 1/(1 − ρ) may still clear 0.1·S_lo.
2. **Relative errors should be certifiable locally even when absolute ones are not.** The error of the difference between neighbouring poses (the local shape of the trajectory) cancels the smooth modes. This could be the paper's angle, if absolute certificates turn out borderline.

## 15:40 · Stereo prefix: the absolute certificates are vacuous at K ≤ 2, and why

Prefix frozen at the normal-run cap: 161 keyframes, 13,617 landmarks, p_L = 1e-6. Run flagged because the pose floor (0.108) is above the 0.05 margin.

| certificate | ρ | valid? |
|---|---|---|
| C1 (classical) | 2.91 | no |
| C2 (backbone) | 2.28 | no |
| C3, K = 1 | 2.15 | no |
| C3, K = 2 | 1.78 | no |
| C3, K = 3 | running | |

C2 used to take more than 30 minutes here. It now takes 56 s: a structured computation that is exact for pose–landmark coupling, checked against the general one to 1e-15.

**Where ρ(A) comes from (K = 1):**
- Pose rows: median row sum 8.05, of which 6.98 comes from landmark columns and 0.93 from pose columns.
- Landmark rows: median 1.42, almost all from other landmarks.
- The largest entries of the Perron vector are poses 145–160 (t = 32–36 s), the same stretch where the D2 pose floor was worst (pose 150).

**Mechanism.**
- A pose's patch boundary contains its GP neighbours, and each of them is coupled to about 300 landmarks outside the patch.
- C3 bounds the leak by summing 300 per-landmark norms (triangle inequality): many small terms. The true leak is one 12-dimensional vector H_{b,E} e_E.
- My scalar prediction (row sums ≈ 1) was too optimistic. It assumed the pose's own landmarks carry the leak, but at K = 1 the GP neighbours' landmarks dominate.

**Consequences and ideas (not yet tested):**
1. *ℓ2 aggregation per boundary node.* Replace Σ_l ‖X_l‖ by the spectral norm of the stacked [X_1 … X_n], and the exterior errors by their ℓ2 aggregate. The comparison becomes nonlinear (c_i = s_i + Σ_b γ_ib ‖c_{N(b)}‖₂) but stays monotone and sublinear. A Collatz–Wielandt argument keeps Theorem 3.5's conclusion u ≤ c* whenever some positive c̄ satisfies F(c̄) ≤ r c̄ with r < 1: if v ≤ F(v), take t = max v_i/c̄_i; then v ≤ t r c̄, so t = 0. Expected gain is up to √(pose dimension) ≈ 3.5, which is probably not enough on its own.
2. *Two-level (coarse-space) certificate.* The component of the error along a small coarse space Z (rigid motions per trajectory segment) is exactly computable from GBP's residual: P_c e = Z(ZᵀHZ)⁻¹Zᵀg, which needs only global sums of local terms. Only the remainder then needs a local certificate. This is the standard remedy for smooth modes in domain decomposition. Open question: how to make the local part exploit the fact that the remainder is H-orthogonal to Z. A deflated operator H + βHZ(ZᵀHZ)⁻¹ZᵀH is exact for it.
3. *Early exit may still be reachable without locality.* S_lo uses the marginal covariance, which includes the drift uncertainty, so the stopping threshold is large exactly where these smooth errors live.

## 16:50 · Landmark certificates by back-substitution (every variable is now certified)

From the landmark row of He = g, e_l = H_ll⁻¹(g_l − Σ_{p∈O(l)} H_lp e_p), and the triangle inequality gives

‖e_l‖_{D_l} ≤ ‖D_l^{-1/2} g_l‖ + Σ_p ‖D_l^{-1/2} H_lp S_pp^{-1/2}‖ E_p.

This is one step with no recursion, so it is valid whenever the pose certificates are. On the prefix frozen at the cap (13,617 landmarks, 21 iterations; `results/phaseC/explore/landmarks_kitti06_prefix_conv.json`):

| pose certificate used | valid everywhere? | E/e median over iterations | E/e 95 % |

## 17:05 · What today's results change about the plan

**1. What we certify.** The brief certifies every variable of the full model: poses, landmarks and markers, with the hub machinery for long tracks. The data say that on real graphs this is valid but vacuous. On stereo ρ(A) stays ≥ 1 at every patch radius, because in the per-block metric each landmark → pose → landmark hop has gain of about 1. **The certificate should be computed on the landmark-marginalized pose model.** Landmark elimination is exact and local: each landmark folds into its own observers. On that model:
- C3 closes at K = 1 (ρ = 0.51);
- it is tight (E/e ≈ 1.0–1.6);
- it certifies early exit within 1–2 points of the oracle.

Landmarks are then certified by back-substitution. This is the method's core, and the likely core of the paper.

**2. What we drop or demote.**
- The hub decomposition (§6 of the brief): on real data it is 1e8× loose because ψ̄ products explode, while the true hub influence is tiny.
- C1 and C2: vacuous everywhere, kept only as the motivating contrast.
- C4: its chain structure is not what limits real graphs.
- Two ideas I tested and rejected: ℓ2 aggregation of the leak, and rigid-motion coarse spaces. The error is neither aligned in a way Cauchy–Schwarz exploits nor dominated by rigid drift.

**3. What the D2 floor means.** The 0.05 margin was meant to guarantee that a floor can never by itself stop a pose from sleeping. The stereo floors, 0.08–0.15 in the reduced metric, exceed the margin but sit far below the real stopping threshold (≈ 0.48; the certified S_lo is about 4.8). **For early exit the floor does not bind.** The p_L sensitivity still stands as a caveat on what "the model's exact solution" means for far landmarks. It is a modelling choice to state in the paper, not a blocker.

**4. Where ChArUco fits.** It is the small-world stress test. Every frame sees the board, so the certificate is exact but global. The paper should say plainly that locality needs landmarks with finite track length.

**5. What Phase D (live early exit) should use.**
- C3 on the reduced pose model, with K = 1 or 2 fixed from the Phase C results.
- The patch solve runs over the pose's covisibility neighbourhood, with the landmarks already marginalized.
- The statistical radius S_lo comes from the patch and Krylov lower bounds on the same reduced model.
- Before building it: locality on KITTI full (running), a per-pose cost comparison against a GBP iteration, and how the certificate is maintained under relinearization. The certificate is per linear model, so a relinearized pose must recompute its patch.

**6. Remaining risks.**
- Patch size. On the prefix a K = 1 covisibility patch is ~70 of 161 poses, because long-track landmarks tie distant keyframes together. KITTI full will show whether this stays bounded (local) or grows with the trajectory.
- The landmark certificates are looser (median ratio ≈ 3).

## 17:56 · How to improve the method: derivations and hypotheses

Notation. Reduced (pose) model: H e = g, one block per pose, D_i = H_ii = C_i C_iᵀ. Patch P_i, exterior E_i. X̃_ij = Σ_{b∈∂P} [H_PP⁻¹]_ib H_bj. The C3 recursion is u ≤ s + A u, with s_i = ‖C_iᵀ [H_PP⁻¹ g_P]_i‖ and a_ij = ‖C_iᵀ X̃_ij C_j⁻ᵀ‖.

### I1. A contraction test each node can check itself, and certificate-driven patches (priority 1)

Fact (Perron–Frobenius / Collatz–Wielandt): for A ≥ 0 and any w > 0, ρ(A) ≤ max_i (A w)_i / w_i. With w = 1, it suffices that **every row sum is ≤ r < 1**. A node checks this from its own row alone, so no global eigenvalue computation is needed, which is what a distributed GBP requires. Moreover:
- c* = (I − A)⁻¹ s satisfies ‖c*‖_∞ ≤ ‖s‖_∞ / (1 − r);
- the monotone iteration c ← s + A c converges at rate r.

Measured maximum row sums on the prefix pose model: 1.031 at K = 1 (ρ = 0.51), 0.168 at K = 2, 0.014 at K = 3. So at K = 1 only a few nodes break the local test.

**Adaptive patches.** Theorem 3.5 allows any patch P_i ∋ i, and the inequality u ≤ s + A u holds for whatever patches are fixed when it is evaluated. So each node can grow its own patch until its row sum is at most r:

    P ← {i};  repeat: solve on P, compute (a_ij);  if Σ_j a_ij ≤ r stop;
                      else add argmax_j a_ij (an exterior block adjacent to ∂P) to P

Validity is untouched and the contraction is certified locally with factor r. Growth can use rank updates of the patch factorization (append one 12 × 12 block row).

**H1.** On KITTI, patches grown to r = 0.5 will be much smaller than K = 1 covisibility patches (median well under 128 poses) and as tight as K = 2 where it matters. Reason: most covisibility couplings are weak (few shared landmarks, far landmarks). A hop counts every covisible pose; the greedy growth adds only the influential ones.

*Test:* greedy patches for r ∈ {0.3, 0.5, 0.8} on the prefix and KITTI full. Report patch size, ρ(A), ratios and stop fractions against K = 1, 2.

### I2. Certify in the statistical (Mahalanobis) metric; the radius becomes exact (priority 2)

The stopping rule compares a D-metric bound with the λ_min-based radius S_lo = √μ_lo · q_d(δ). It is worst case over directions on both sides.

Alternative. Under the Gaussian model x̄_i − x*_i ~ N(0, Σ_ii), so ‖x̄_i − x*_i‖_{Σ_ii⁻¹} ~ χ_d **exactly**. Its (1 − δ) quantile is q_d(δ), with no lower bound needed. Take a certified L_i ⪯ Σ_ii (the patch bound [H_PP⁻¹]_ii, nearly exact at K = 2) and W_i := L_i⁻¹. Then

    ‖e_i‖_{Σ_ii⁻¹} ≤ ‖e_i‖_{W_i}      (because L_i ⪯ Σ_ii implies L_i⁻¹ ⪰ Σ_ii⁻¹).

- Theorem 3.5 holds verbatim with any SPD metric per block. Its proof uses only the triangle inequality and operator norms. So one can compute E_i^W ≥ ‖e_i‖_{W_i} with s_i^W = ‖W_i^{1/2}[H_PP⁻¹ g_P]_i‖ and a_ij^W = ‖W_i^{1/2} X̃_ij W_j^{-1/2}‖.
- **Stopping rule: E_i^W ≤ η q_d(δ).** By Theorem 5.1 in this metric it guarantees that the remaining change is at most an η-fraction of the exact (1 − δ) statistical radius, measured in the statistical metric itself.
- Relation to the brief's rule: ‖e‖_{W_i} ≤ ‖e‖_{D_i} / √λ_min(D_i^{1/2} L_i D_i^{1/2}) = ‖e‖_{D_i} / √μ_lo. With the converted certificate E^W := E^D / √μ_lo the two rules are identical. A certificate computed *directly* in the W-metric helps only if its recursion is not looser than the D-metric one. That has to be measured; there is no free lunch. The gain appears when GBP's remaining error lies in poorly determined directions (along-track translation, yaw), where slow GBP modes live, *and* the W-recursion keeps that directional information.
- The contraction ρ(A^W) can differ from ρ(A^D). It has to be measured.

**H2.** Mahalanobis certificates computed directly in W stop poses earlier, by a factor up to the anisotropy √(λ_max/λ_min) of D^{1/2} Σ D^{1/2}, whenever ρ(A^W) is comparable to ρ(A^D). Validity is never at stake.

### I3. Cost is amortized per linearization (to state in the paper)

Per model (per relinearization), each node factorizes H_{P_i P_i} once and stores Y_i = [H_PP⁻¹]_{:,i} (|P_i| × 12) and its row of A. Per GBP iteration, s_i = ‖C_iᵀ Y_iᵀ g_{P_i}‖ is one |P_i| × 12 matrix-vector product, and the fixed point c = s + A c is a sparse, message-like iteration contracting at rate r. For KITTI at K = 1 that is about 477 × 1,536 × 12 ≈ 9e6 flops per iteration, negligible next to a GBP sweep. The factorization cost, about |P|³/3 ≈ 1e9 flops per pose, is paid once per relinearization. **Early exit pays whenever relinearization is less frequent than a few tens of iterations.**

### I4. Tighter landmark certificates: aggregate over observers jointly (priority 3)

Today, ‖e_l‖ ≤ ‖C_l⁻¹ g_l‖ + Σ_{p∈O(l)} ‖C_l⁻¹ H_lp S_p^{-1/2}‖ E_p, with median ratio about 3. Two tighter bounds:
- (a) Cauchy–Schwarz over the k observers: ‖C_l⁻¹ H_{l,O} e_O‖ ≤ ‖C_l⁻¹ H_{l,O} blkdiag(S_p)^{-1/2}‖₂ · (Σ_p E_p²)^{1/2}. The gain is at most √k (k ≈ 5), because a 3-dimensional landmark receives k differently oriented pulls.
- (b) A joint bound on the stacked observer error. At K = 1 the observers of a landmark all lie in each other's patches, so one patch solve bounds e_O jointly: the local term s_O = ‖S_OO^{1/2}[H_PP⁻¹ g_P]_O‖ plus a leak term, which keeps the correlation between observers.

**H4.** (b) brings the landmark ratio from about 3 toward the pose ratio (1.0–1.5).

### I5. A partially reduced model: short tracks eliminated, long tracks as hubs

Eliminating every landmark turns a long track into a long-range pose coupling, and those couplings make K = 1 patches large (70–128 poses). Instead, eliminate only landmarks whose track spans ≤ w poses. The pose model is then banded (block-tridiagonal in supernodes of w poses; Lemma 4.1), and the remaining long-track landmarks form a hub set S, exactly the structure of PROOF_NOTE §4, now without short-track landmark rows.
- The chain lemmas (§1) and C4 apply to the banded pose model with certified decay ψ̄ per supernode link.
- On stereo, ψ̄ < 1 is plausible: strong visual information per keyframe and 0.2 s spacing. On ChArUco it failed for a specific reason (30 fps, velocity only through the GP prior).
- Deciding data: the ψ̄ distribution from the full-model C4 run now in progress, and a direct measurement on the banded reduced model.
- Risk: the hub bound (Theorem 4.6) was 1e8× loose on ChArUco. It must be retested on stereo, where hubs are 3-dimensional points seen for tens of frames, not a 6-dof board seen everywhere.

### I6. A certifiable GBP variant: re-centre two regularizers (solver change, for the paper)

Phase A attributed GBP's fixed-point bias to ε3 (marginalization) and ε4 (factor-to-variable message). Both are centred at cavity means, which are GBP-internal quantities. If they are instead centred at the frozen linearization point (tangent 0), every regularizer becomes a fixed prior N(0, ε⁻¹ I) on a known subspace. GBP's fixed point is then the exact solution of H + G_ε for a known G_ε, which can be exported and certified. The floor becomes rounding level, and the D2 problem with weak landmarks disappears: their weak directions are pinned by a known prior instead of drifting with the cavity.

**H6.** With re-centred ε3/ε4 the frozen floor falls from 5e-4–7e-4 (ChArUco) and 0.08–0.15 (stereo, pose metric) to below 1e-9, with no change in convergence rate (ε is small).

This changes message equations, which the brief allows only after Phase D. It would be a separate variant, clearly labelled.

### I7. Live early exit under relinearization (Phase D design)

A certificate is valid for one linear model. G-solver relinearizes every factor every iteration (F1), so the model changes each iteration and the certificate would need refactorization each time.

Proposal: relinearize in epochs of T iterations (freeze, iterate, refreeze). Within an epoch the certificates are exact. Across N epochs, Theorem 5.2(b) allocates δ/N per epoch; for N bounded in advance, only the quantile changes, from q_d(δ) to q_d(δ/N), and that change is slow: q_12(0.005) = 5.32 and q_12(0.0005) = 5.90 against q_12(0.05) = 4.59. This matters for statements holding simultaneously over all epochs.

**H7.** Epochs of T ≈ 20–50 iterations barely change GBP's trajectory, because Phase A's frozen and unfrozen runs agree to O(model change). Certified early exit then saves most node updates against the default schedule.

### I8. "Directly from GBP": messages as boundary conditions (longer term)

For a patch P the exact identity is (H_PP − Ŝ_E) e_P = g_P − H_PE H_EE⁻¹ g_E, where Ŝ_E = H_PE H_EE⁻¹ H_EP is the exterior's Schur complement on ∂P. On trees GBP's converged messages into P equal Ŝ_E (F4, corrected); on loopy graphs they approximate it. If the approximation error were certified, M̂ − Ŝ_E ⪯ Δ, a patch solve with GBP's messages as boundary conditions would replace C3's recursion. The leak would then shrink to a far-field residual term plus Δ. This is attractive, since it reuses GBP's own messages, but it needs a new bound on Δ. For now it is a hypothesis, not a plan.

### Order of the next experiments

1. I1 (greedy local patches). Cheapest and aimed at the main weakness, patch size.
2. I2 (Mahalanobis metric). Changes only the metric; directly tests earlier stopping.
3. I4(a)/(b) (landmarks).
4. I5 (banded reduced model and hubs), informed by the running C4 ψ̄.
5. I7 (epochs) as the Phase D design; I6 as a labelled solver variant.

Decision rule for each: adopt it only if (i) every invariant still passes, (ii) the certified stop fraction moves toward the oracle, and (iii) the patch size or cost does not grow.
